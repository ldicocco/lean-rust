Part II is about the central discovery of the subject: proofs and programs are
the same thing. It starts with the untyped λ-calculus, the smallest programming
language there is, then adds simple types and meets Rust's closures along the
way. It proves, completely and mechanically in Lean, that well-typed programs
do not go wrong. With that in hand, the Curry–Howard correspondence stops being
a slogan: propositions are types, proofs are programs, and simplifying a proof
is running a program. The part ends with the algebra of types, where sums,
products and functions obey the laws of arithmetic.
