Part IV steps back and looks at the structure that all the previous chapters
share. Category theory is the mathematics of composition: objects, arrows between
them, and laws about how arrows combine. Types and functions form a category,
and so do propositions and proofs. Functors explain `map`, natural
transformations explain why generic functions are so well behaved, cartesian
closed categories are what the simply typed λ-calculus *is*, and monads explain
`?`, `and_then` and `do` notation. The definitions are written in Lean, with the
laws as proof obligations, and every instance is proved lawful.
